# 2 Trail — Problem Statement
*Problem → Users → Current Behavior → Pain Points → Core Problem → Opportunity → Problem Statement*

## 01 — The Problem: Discovery is Built Around Destinations, Not Journeys
People can easily find places through search engines, maps, social media, blogs and recommendations. But finding individual destinations is not the same as deciding how those places become a meaningful journey.

- **The Gap**: Finding a place is easy. Connecting places into a worthwhile journey requires additional planning.
- **The Current Experience**: Search → Find places → Compare → Save → Manually plan → Navigate.
- **The TRAIL Question**: Instead of *"Where should I go?"*, TRAIL asks: *"What can I discover along the way?"*

---

## 02 — Who Experiences This? (Target Users)
### Primary: Curious Young Explorers
Students and young adults who:
- Have limited time to explore
- Enjoy discovering new places
- May not have a fixed destination
- Want less-obvious places
- Don't want to spend significant time planning
- Explore independently or with friends

### Secondary: Local Explorers & Creators
People who:
- Know interesting local places
- Enjoy creating routes
- Want to share experiences
- Can contribute local knowledge and curated trails

---

## 03 — How People Explore Today (The Current Solution)
1. **01 Search (Google Maps, Search)**: Find places by location, category and popularity.
2. **02 Discover (Instagram, YouTube, Reddit, Blogs)**: Find recommendations, photos, experiences and ideas.
3. **03 Compare (Reviews, Images, Distance, Hours)**: Judge personal relevance.
4. **04 Assemble (Manual Effort)**: Which places? What order? How much time? What to skip? The user decides manually.
*Existing tools help users find places. The user still has to build the journey.*

---

## 04 — The Friction (Seven Pain-Point Hypotheses)
1. **Destination-Centric**: Focus is on individual places rather than how places connect.
2. **Popularity-Driven**: Popular places dominate, making less-obvious ones harder to surface.
3. **Fragmented**: Discovery is spread across multiple platforms.
4. **Manual Journey Planning**: Users decide which places belong together and in what order.
5. **Time Constraints**: Hard to know what realistically fits into an outing.
6. **Missed Discoveries**: Interesting places may be overlooked if outside the original destination.
7. **Limited Spontaneity**: Heavy planning reduces the chance of unexpected discovery.

---

## 05 — The Core Problem
**Finding places is not the same as building a journey.**
Today a person may find: Café · Bookstore · Street Art · Bakery · Viewpoint.
But they still have to ask:
- Which ones should I visit?
- In what order?
- How long will the journey take?
- Which places fit my interests?
- Which discoveries are worth a detour?

> **The Core Problem Statement**:
> Discovery tools make it easier to locate individual places, but connecting them into a meaningful journey can still require significant manual effort.

---

## 06 — The Opportunity: From Destination to Journey
TRAIL explores an opportunity to make discovery more journey-oriented:
- **User Inputs**: Location, Interests, Time, Distance.
- **TRAIL Connects**: Places based on relevance, distance, sequence and context.
- **Outcome**: A sequenced pedestrian discovery journey.
- **Core Hypothesis**: Users need an easier way to discover and combine interesting places into a journey based on their interests, available time and preferred distance.

---

## 07 — The Opportunity in One Statement
- **Problem**: People can easily discover individual destinations across maps, search engines and social platforms, but turning those discoveries into a coherent, worthwhile journey requires fragmented research and manual planning.
- **User**: Curious young explorers with limited time who want to discover interesting or less-obvious places without spending significant effort planning.
- **Need**: A simpler way to connect relevant places into a journey based on interests, available time, location and preferred distance.
- **Opportunity**: Shift discovery from *“Where should I go?”* to *“What can I discover along the way?”*

> **DESTINATION → JOURNEY → TRAIL**
> **DON'T JUST GET THERE. DISCOVER ALONG THE WAY.**
