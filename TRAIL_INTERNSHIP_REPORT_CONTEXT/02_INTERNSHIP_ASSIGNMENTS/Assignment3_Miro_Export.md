# Assignment 03: Business Model Analysis — Netflix, Uber & IKEA
*(Miro Export / Strategic Synthesis)*

## Conceptual Framework: What is a Business Model?
> “A business model describes the rationale of how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value.”
> — Alexander Osterwalder & Yves Pigneur (*Business Model Generation*). 
> It is not merely how a company makes money, but the entire operating logic connecting product development, distribution channels, cost realities, and customer retention.

1. **Value Creation (What problem is solved?)**: Identifying unmet customer needs, designing compelling offerings, and creating distinct utility through intellectual property, platform algorithms, or physical product design.
2. **Value Delivery (How does it reach users?)**: The logistics, distribution channels, customer touchpoints, and technical infrastructure required to place the product or service seamlessly into the customer's hands.
3. **Value Capture (How is money earned?)**: The monetization architecture (subscription fees, take-rates, retail margins) that recovers operating costs, funds reinvestment, and generates sustainable profit.

## The Three Company Archetypes in This Study

### 1. Netflix — Subscription Model (SVOD)
- **Core Archetype**: Direct-to-Consumer SVOD Subscription
- **Core Asset**: Proprietary content library & streaming algorithms
- **Asset Dependency**: Near-zero physical delivery assets; marginal distribution cost approaches zero
- **Revenue Engine**: Predictable recurring monthly membership fees across multiple pricing tiers + ad-supported tier
- **Cost Structure**: Heavy upfront content amortization & production ($14B–$17B annually), AWS cloud infrastructure
- **Scalability Lever**: Global digital reach; adding an extra viewer adds virtually zero delivery cost
- **FY2024 Financial Performance**: $39.00 Billion Total Revenue (+16% YoY), $10.42B Operating Income (26.7% Operating Margin), 301.6M Global Subscribers

### 2. Uber — Platform / Marketplace Model
- **Core Archetype**: Asset-Light Two-Sided Marketplace
- **Core Asset**: Matching & routing algorithms, two-sided network liquidity & trust systems
- **Asset Dependency**: Asset-light; does not own vehicle fleets or employ drivers directly
- **Revenue Engine**: Transactional platform take-rate (~27.1% overall in FY2024) deducted per completed trip/order
- **Cost Structure**: Commercial auto insurance, payment processing, platform tech & incentives
- **Scalability Lever**: Network effects; increasing local liquidity improves wait times and driver earnings
- **FY2024 Financial Performance**: $162.0 Billion Gross Bookings, $43.98 Billion Net Revenue, $2.80B GAAP Operating Income, $6.9B Free Cash Flow, 150M+ Monthly Active Users

### 3. IKEA — Physical Retail & Integrated Supply Chain
- **Core Archetype**: Vertically Integrated Omnichannel Retailer
- **Core Asset**: Global supplier network, flat-pack packaging IP & destination warehouse stores
- **Asset Dependency**: Asset-heavy physical logistics, massive real estate, raw materials & inventory
- **Revenue Engine**: Direct product gross margin from high-volume home furnishing sales + Swedish Food Market
- **Cost Structure**: Raw materials, maritime/truck freight, store staffing & warehouse maintenance
- **Scalability Lever**: Extreme supply-chain scale, flat-pack container density & customer self-assembly
- **FY2024 Financial Performance**: €45.1 Billion Total Retail Sales across all franchisees, €2.1B invested in price cuts, 400+ stores & 174 order points

## Key Strategic Takeaways
1. **Value capture must mirror value delivery**: Utility consumption pattern determines revenue model.
2. **Orchestration can replace asset ownership**: Asset-light platforms coordinate physical utility without balance-sheet capital.
3. **The reality of scale**: Digital scale shifts expenses to upfront fixed capital; physical retail optimizes marginal supply-chain density.
4. **A business model is an integrated virtuous system**: True competitive advantage arises when all 9 blocks reinforce each other.
